# Mini Dash: dash-top speed, G-force and EV display for a 2022 Mini Cooper SE

This file is the single source of truth for the project. Read it fully before doing anything. When a decision changes, update this file in the same commit.

Owner: Logan. Orchestration and research happen in Claude chat; CAD-as-code and firmware happen here in Claude Code. Australian English, "program" not "programme", no em dashes in docs or UI strings.

Version 2 brief, 23 Oct 2026. The original plan split GPS/IMU (phase 1) from OBD (phase 2). Logan has decided to build the OBD input into the first release, so this brief covers both. Bring-up is still staged (section 10) because the OBD path cannot be tested until the dongle arrives, but the architecture, data model and UI are designed for the full feature set from the start.

## 1. What we are building

A dash-top device for a 2022 Mini Cooper SE (F56 electric) that shows live speed, lateral and longitudinal G, acceleration and braking timers, trip stats, and the car's own EV data (battery state of charge, power in and out, battery and motor temperatures, torque, pedal, wheel speeds) on a 4.3" touch IPS screen. Powered from the car's USB-C port. GPS and IMU are inside the device on one I2C bus; the car data arrives over Bluetooth LE from a vLinker BM+ dongle in the OBD port. Printed enclosure in ASA or PETG, no supports, no glue; the device clips onto a thin adhesive plate on the dash and lifts off to be stowed. No custom PCB, no soldering, no car wiring beyond USB-C and the dongle.

Finished device about 122 x 100 x 61 mm including the visor and dovetail rail (the lens alone is 112.4 x 75.1 mm). It sits on the dash top directly above the instrument cluster, where the HUD combiner would be on HUD-equipped cars (this car has none), tilted back toward the driver. Fallback position: dash top between the cluster hood and the centre dial.

### Hardware facts that constrain the design

- The RGB panel consumes almost every GPIO. Exposed on the 4.3C: I2C on GPIO8 (SDA) / GPIO9 (SCL), shared with touch (0x5D), the IO expander (0x24), the RTC (0x51) and the ES8311 codec (read its address from the schematic, 0x18 is typical); isolated DI/DO through the expander; TF card on GPIO11/12/13. There is no free UART, so all sensors are on the one I2C bus, and the car data comes in over BLE.
- Expected I2C map: 0x10 VEML7700, 0x18 ES8311 codec (Waveshare es8311_codec.h, 0x30 in 8-bit form), 0x24 IO expander, 0x40 ES7210 microphone ADC (es7210_adc.h, 0x80 in 8-bit form), 0x42 NEO-M9N, 0x51 PCF85063A, 0x5D GT911, 0x6B ISM330DHCX. Run a scan at bring-up and record the real map in `board_config.h`; stop and ask if anything unexpected appears. Bus at 400 kHz.
- VOUT on the I2C terminal must read 3.3 V with a multimeter before any sensor is connected. All three sensors are 3.3 V parts.
- The car's forward axis in the IMU's frame depends on how the IMU board is mounted, so it is a stored setting (serial key A), not a constant: +Y flat on the bench, -Z in the finished enclosure, where the board stands upright facing the driver. The firmware refuses a setting that leaves the axis within 30 degrees of vertical for the mounting (24 Nov 2026).
- Every I2C access, including LVGL's touch read, the IO expander (backlight PWM) and the RTC, goes through one FreeRTOS mutex with a bounded hold time. No driver may touch the bus outside it.
- The ESP32-S3 has BLE only, no Bluetooth Classic. The dongle's BLE side is a GATT serial service (on this dongle service 0x18F0 with notify characteristic 0x2AF0 and write characteristic 0x2AF1, found 24 Nov 2026; the client discovers the pair rather than assuming it). WiFi and BLE share the S3's radio; keep WiFi off while driving and only enable it in the OTA settings mode.
- Toolchain pinned from the 4.3C repo's `config/ci.json`: Arduino core 3.3.11, FQBN options `USBMode=hwcdc, CDCOnBoot=cdc, CPUFreq=240, FlashMode=qio, FlashSize=16M, PartitionScheme=app3M_fat9M_16MB, PSRAM=opi, UploadSpeed=921600`. In PlatformIO use `board = esp32-s3-devkitc-1` with these as board overrides, on the pioarduino platform release 55.03.311 (the official espressif32 platform stopped at Arduino core 2.x), with PlatformIO itself on Python 3.10 to 3.14 (set up 24 Oct 2026, see firmware/README.md). The default env minidash-xip has pioarduino rebuild the core from ESP-IDF with CONFIG_SPIRAM_XIP_FROM_PSRAM, CONFIG_SPIRAM_FETCH_INSTRUCTIONS, CONFIG_SPIRAM_RODATA and CONFIG_LCD_RGB_ISR_IRAM_SAFE: on the prebuilt core every flash write stalls the RGB panel's PSRAM refill and the glass tears (24 Oct 2026). Stay on one env; switching envs rebuilds the libraries for three minutes. Build and flash Waveshare's own LCD example first; only then build ours. Wrong PSRAM or flash mode is the classic cause of "flashed fine, screen black".
- Flashing: the single USB-C port is native USB, auto-reset normally works. Recovery: hold BOOT, press and release RESET, release BOOT, flash, press RESET. Factory image restore: `esptool --chip esp32s3 erase-flash` then `write-flash 0x0 firmware/ESP32-S3-Touch-LCD-4.3C-Test.bin` from the 4.3C repo.
- Links: docs https://docs.waveshare.com/ESP32-S3-Touch-LCD-4.3C, GitHub https://github.com/waveshareteam/ESP32-S3-Touch-LCD-4.3C (schematic in `hardware/schematics/`, 2D PDF, DXF and STEP in `hardware/dimensions/`, IO expander driver in the ESP-IDF examples, factory image in `firmware/`). SparkFun NEO-M9N hardware: https://github.com/sparkfun/SparkFun_u-Blox_NEO_M9N.

## 3. Car data over OBD (new in v2)

### What the Cooper SE exposes

Almost none of it is standard OBD. It is BMW UDS "ReadDataByIdentifier" (service 0x22) traffic to individual ECUs. Two open projects have mapped it and are the references: OVMS `vehicle_minise` (working C++ on ESP32, read-only, derived from their BMW i3 module, https://docs.openvehicles.com/en/latest/components/vehicle_minise/docs/index.html, source in the OVMS v3 repo under `vehicle/OVMS.V3/components/vehicle_minise`) and the OBDb MINI Cooper parameter list (https://pelican.clutch.engineering/cars/mini/cooper/, repo github.com/OBDb/MINI-Cooper). Pull ECU addresses, DIDs, scaling and units from those sources into `firmware/include/obd_pids.h` with a comment citing where each came from. Do not invent DIDs.

Metrics to read, in priority order (first-release set marked *):

- * HV battery SOC: the battery ECU's display SOC, shown as it is. Checked against the car 24 Nov 2026: 50.7 from the ECU, 51 on the dash. The maximum and minimum the same identifier returns are not usable limits to scale between (99.9 and 14.5 on this car), so nothing is scaled
- * HV battery voltage and current; power kW = V x I / 1000, positive discharging
- * Battery temperature min and max; cell voltage avg/min/max (spread = max minus min)
- * Motor speed, actual torque, requested torque; motor temperatures 1 and 2
- * Accelerator pedal position
- * Wheel speeds (four) and their average; this is the true-speedo speed, display it alongside GPS speed
- * 12 V battery voltage
- Inverter and drive-electronics temperatures, DC-DC board temperature
- State of health, battery capacity
- Range by mode, consumption per 100 km, auxiliary power
- Charging telemetry (plug, cable rating, AC per phase, DC V and I, charger temperature, derating). Only when stationary; a "charging" page is optional and last.
- Not available from the car per OVMS: per-cell voltages, trip regen kWh (compute regen from current integration instead).

### How it is read

- BLE central on the ESP32-S3 (NimBLE, not Bluedroid: smaller and more stable with LVGL and PSRAM; the Arduino core 3.3 is built with the NimBLE host, and its own BLE library runs on it, so no extra library). Built 24 Nov 2026 as `firmware/src/app/obd_client.*` (transport, session, schedule), `obd_proto.*` (ELM reply parser) and `obd_decode.*` (bounds-checked decoding), the last two tested natively against replies recorded from cars. The stack takes 72 KB of internal RAM, which is why LVGL's object pool lives in PSRAM. Scan for the dongle by name prefix "vLinker", connect, discover the serial service, subscribe to notify, write AT and UDS commands. Pairing is a deliberate act (Logan, 24 Nov 2026): the dash looks for a new dongle only after a Pair tap in Settings while parked, stores it only once the car has answered through it, and with no dongle stored and no tap connects to nothing; the stored dongle (one NVS record: address, type, name) is the only one it reconnects to. Still to build: showing the name it found for confirmation before storing it.
- ELM327 setup sequence: `ATZ`, `ATE0`, `ATL0`, `ATS0`, `ATH1`, `ATSP6` (ISO 15765-4 CAN 11-bit 500 kbit) or `ATSP7` as the BMW references require, `ATAT1`. BMW F-series diagnostics use a tester ID of 0x6F1 with extended addressing: `ATSH 6F1`, `ATCEA xx` (xx = target ECU address), `ATCRA 6xx` (response filter), `ATFCSH 6F1`, `ATFCSD xx 30 00 00` (flow control must carry the ECU address byte), `ATFCSM 1`. Then send `22 DD DD` requests and parse the `62 DD DD ...` replies. ECU addresses from the OVMS source and OBDb (both agree): SME battery 0x07, EME drive electronics 0x1A, EDM powertrain 0x12, DSC 0x29, KOM cluster 0x60, NBT head unit 0x63. The identifiers in use, with scaling and a citation each, are in `firmware/include/obd_pids.h`.
- Poll scheduling: fast group (voltage, current, torque, pedal, wheel speeds) at 5 Hz as a round-robin, slow group (temperatures, SOC limits, 12 V) at 0.5 Hz, static group (SOH, capacity, counts) once per connection. Bound each request with a 300 ms timeout; after three consecutive timeouts drop to a reconnect state. As built: changing ECU costs three adapter commands, so a visit asks one ECU for everything due and then moves to the ECU with the most overdue identifier; three requests the car leaves unanswered mean "car asleep" (one probe every 1.5 s), three the dongle itself ignores mean reconnect. Measured in the car 24 Nov 2026: a request costs 45 to 130 ms and a change of ECU about 50 ms, so the schedule gives battery power and wheel speed 1.9 Hz each, torque and pedal about 0.5 Hz and the slow group 0.5 Hz; 5 Hz for the whole fast group is not reachable through this adapter. Wheel speeds come from the DSC (0.01 km/h, OBDb only) when the car answers, else from the head unit (1 km/h, OVMS).
- The car's buses are awake with ignition on and for about 40 s after. The device shows "car asleep" when the dongle connects but requests time out; it never blocks the GPS/IMU UI on OBD.
- Treat everything the dongle returns as untrusted input: bounds-check every decoded value against physical limits before display or storage, and never let a malformed frame crash the parser.

## 5. Firmware architecture

- PlatformIO, Arduino framework, core and board options as pinned in section 2. LVGL 8.4 with Waveshare's `lv_conf.h` as the starting point; ESP32_Display_Panel for the RGB panel and GT911; the ported IO expander driver for backlight and touch reset.
- Tasks: core 1 runs LVGL (5 ms tick) and nothing else heavy. Core 0 runs the sensor task (I2C: IMU FIFO at 208 Hz, GPS PVT at 10 Hz, lux at 1 Hz) and the OBD task (NimBLE client, poll scheduler, parser). All three share a single `VehicleState` struct protected by a mutex, with a monotonic timestamp on every field so the UI can grey out stale values (GPS stale after 500 ms, OBD fast group after 1 s, slow group after 5 s; since 24 Nov 2026 every car measurement has its own stamp, so one reply cannot keep another looking fresh). The Arduino loop task is the only writer of trips, records, traces and settings (`firmware/src/app/persist.*`, 24 Nov 2026): the UI, the sensor task and the trip engine leave requests or finished traces and never touch flash, and the engine is read and rotated only under the state lock, with the copy written after the lock is released.
- Attitude and gravity (done 25 Oct 2026, see docs/decisions.md for the gains; accelerometer offsets calibrated once per sensor with a sphere fit, docs/calibration.md): static mounting calibration is not enough (1 degree of pitch error projects 0.017 g into the longitudinal axis). Keep a gyro-propagated quaternion at 208 Hz, corrected slowly toward the accelerometer gravity vector only when near-stationary or in steady motion (GPS speed stable, low gyro rates), never during sustained acceleration or cornering. Vehicle-frame G = rotate(accel) minus gravity. Estimate accelerometer bias at standstill. Added 24 Nov 2026 after the independent review: the accelerometer is also held off while speed times yaw rate (the centripetal acceleration, known without the accelerometer) exceeds 0.05 g, released after 0.5 s under 0.03 g, because a long gentle bend passes every other test and the lateral g then fades to zero (starting thresholds, to be tuned from the first logged drive); the gyro bias is learnt only when GPS says the car is standing; the offset calibration is applied only if the fit is within 10 mg RMS and 20 mg for its worst direction.
- Three speed/G paths: (1) displayed speed = GPS Doppler speed fused with integrated longitudinal accel in a complementary filter, time-aligned on UBX iTOW; when OBD wheel speed is live and fresh, it becomes the display source and GPS is the cross-check; (2) timing path = raw GPS speed samples with crossing-time interpolation, no smoothing, unchanged by OBD; (3) G graphics = 2nd-order low-pass about 5 Hz, peak hold with slow decay, ring buffer.
- Timers and records: fun and personal comparison, tolerance about 0.1 s. km/h thresholds, mph display only. Arm when GPS speed under 1 km/h and accel under 0.05 g for 1 s with 3D fix, 7 or more sats, speed accuracy under 0.5 m/s. Start on fused speed crossing 1 km/h; no rollout. Target crossings interpolated between timestamped samples. Invalidate on fix loss, degraded accuracy or a missed GPS epoch. The braking timer starts on a downward crossing of 60 km/h under braking and records time and distance to a standstill; a rise in speed on the way cancels it. Decided by Logan 24 Nov 2026 and still to be implemented with the rest of the timer rework: the record is a continuous braking stop, at least 0.1 g of braking throughout, and coasting for more than 2 s in total cancels it, so a coast down to a stop is never a record. The timers are to be rebuilt on GPS epochs (they run on a 20 Hz tick with a cached fix today) once the first capture log shows the real epoch timing; until then the device runs in capture mode. 60-100 is a rolling run detected on its own. Peak battery draw and regen are one-second means (instantaneous maxima kept alongside); G peaks come from the 5 Hz filtered path, combined G from the same sample, with shock spikes rejected. Every record carries the trip and event id, timestamp, SOC, pack temperature, source, validity and a 10 s trace window (ring buffer, saved on acceptance). A record is accepted once; comparisons name their baseline.
- Night mode: VEML7700 lux with hysteresis (below 15 lux for 5 s enters, above 40 lux for 5 s exits) drives real backlight PWM through the expander, plus a dark palette. Manual override in Settings.
- Capture mode (`firmware/src/app/capture.*`, 24 Nov 2026): a stored switch, on by default until the timers have been checked against a logged drive (serial MM switches it and restarts). In it timer runs and peaks are logged as candidates and never become records, no trip, record or trace is written, what is stored is left untouched and the replay is refused. Separately, serial key C starts a raw stream over USB (every GPS epoch with its arrival time, every IMU sample, the gate decisions, the engine's inputs, every OBD exchange, task stack margins; no latitude or longitude) which `firmware/tools/drive_log.py` records and `capture_parse.py` splits and summarises.
- State: NVS (Preferences) for calibration, settings, dongle address and all-time records, with atomic writes (write, then commit a version key). Files are replaced safely since 24 Nov 2026: written to a second file, synced, read back and compared, then renamed over the old one (the LittleFS port in this build empties a file the moment it is opened for writing, so the earlier belief that a replace was atomic on close was wrong); trip and event files carry a CRC-32; the checkpoint is one file per trip under /active and is removed only once its trip file is safely written; a partition that holds data is never formatted because a mount failed (serial KK is the one deliberate format). Flash writes are immediate on the rebuilt core (env minidash-xip): event traces on acceptance, the checkpoint every 30 s. On the prebuilt core (env minidash) they are deferred to standstills because a write stalls the panel refill there. Event files whose trip never got a trip file are pruned at boot. A LittleFS partition on the internal flash (the 9 MB the app3M_fat9M scheme sets aside) for bounded per-trip summaries (last 60 trips, which is what Today and All time and the last-trip comparisons read), event trace windows and charge sessions; the active trip is checkpointed every 30 s. Nothing on the TF card. A trip starts at boot (the device is powered by the car) and ends at power loss; the checkpoint makes a power cut lose at most 30 s. The first checkpoint is written when the trip reaches 50 m; before that the trip does not exist as far as storage and the records are concerned.
- Boot: dash visible within 1 s target, before any sensor or BLE is ready. Explicit "acquiring" and "connecting" states. Timers locked until fix gates pass. BLE scan starts at boot and retries with backoff; it must never delay the UI.
- Power loss is a reboot; only an in-progress timer run and up to 30 s of trip integration are lost. The device is powered from the car's USB-C and is off whenever the car is parked or charging (Logan, 24 Oct 2026); there is no sleep mode, no battery and no charging telemetry.

## 8. Working conventions

- Small commits, each buildable. `pio run` and `firmware/test/native/run.sh` (the native tests: speed fusion, attitude, calibration, OBD protocol, trip engine, capture format) must pass before any firmware commit; `python mini_dash_enclosure.py export/` must succeed before any enclosure commit.
- Pin names, I2C addresses and board facts live in `firmware/include/board_config.h`; OBD ECU addresses, DIDs and scaling live in `firmware/include/obd_pids.h` with a source citation per line. Nowhere else.
- When a hardware fact is uncertain, check the schematic, the 4.3C repo or the OVMS/OBDb sources in `hardware/datasheets/` rather than guessing; if still unsure, stop and ask Logan.
- A statement that something works says how it was checked: on the device, natively, or by building only. The independent review of 24 Nov 2026 found claims of mine (an atomic file replace, a stall measurement, a verified speed fusion) that had not been checked the way they were worded; `docs/review-findings.md` is the ledger.
- Log decisions in `docs/decisions.md` with a date and one line of reasoning. Log every OBD request/response pair seen during bring-up to `docs/obd-notes.md`.
- Keep UI strings short; the screen is read at a glance while driving. Nothing on screen requires more than one tap while moving.

## 9. Acceptance tests (pass/fail)

Record results in `docs/test-log.md`. Nothing is "done" until it has a dated pass.

- Power: measured current at full backlight with GPS, antenna and BLE live, plus boot peak; the Mini's USB-C port holds 4.75 to 5.25 V under that load through the chosen cable.
- Sensor supply: VOUT on the I2C terminal measures 3.3 V before any sensor is connected.
- I2C: a scan shows every expected address and no unexpected one; 30 minutes of simultaneous touch, GPS at 10 Hz and IMU FIFO reads with zero bus errors.
- Display: readable at arm's length at midday with sun on the windscreen, visor fitted; at night the backlight dims to a non-glare level with no visible flicker; measured brightness recorded.
- Startup: dash visible within 1 s; "acquiring" and "connecting" states shown; hot-start fix in under 10 s on the dash.
- GPS: internal patch antenna gives 8 or more satellites and speed accuracy under 0.3 m/s on an open road; else relocate the antenna, then fall back to the external puck.
- BLE/OBD: connects to the dongle within 10 s of ignition on; fast group holds 5 Hz with under 2 percent timeouts over a 20 minute drive; reconnects unaided after a bus sleep/wake cycle; power kW sign and magnitude agree with the car's own power gauge; wheel-speed average agrees with the speedo; SOC matches the dash within 1 percent.
- Mount: no movement relative to the plate under a hard stop from 60 km/h; IMU zero shifts under 0.02 g over a drive.
- Timing: three back-to-back 0-60 runs on the same road agree within 0.1 s; displayed GPS speed within the speedo's usual 2 to 3 km/h over-read; with OBD live, wheel speed and GPS speed agree within 2 km/h at steady 60.
- Power loss: 20 abrupt power cuts, including during a settings write, leave calibration, settings, dongle address and best runs intact.
- 12 V drain: with the dongle left in for 7 days of not driving, the car starts normally.
- Thermal: after 20 minutes in the sun with the device running, screen readable and touch working.
